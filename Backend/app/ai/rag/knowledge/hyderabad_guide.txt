Hyderabad, the City of Pearls and capital of Telangana, is an iconic blend of historic royal heritage and modern technology. Founded in 1591 by Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah along the Musi River, the city was ruled by the Qutb Shahi dynasty and later the Nizams of Asaf Jahi dynasty, who made it one of the wealthiest princely states in India.

== Understand ==

=== History ===
Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah established Hyderabad in 1591 to escape water shortages and epidemics in the fortified capital of Golconda. The iconic Charminar was built at the center of the new city to commemorate the eradication of a deadly plague. The Nizams ruled Hyderabad from 1724 until 1948, fostering an opulent court culture of Urdu poetry, exquisite culinary traditions, pearl trading, and monumental architecture including the Chowmahalla Palace and Falaknuma Palace. In modern times, the city expanded into HITEC City (Cyberabad), becoming a premier global information technology and biotechnology hub.

=== Climate ===
Hyderabad features a tropical wet and dry to semi-arid climate. 
- Summer (March to May): Hot and dry, with daily temperatures often exceeding 40°C (104°F). Light cotton clothing and midday shade are essential.
- Monsoon (June to September): Heavy rainfall and thunderstorms with moderate temperatures around 28°C–32°C.
- Winter (October to February): The best time to visit, featuring pleasant sunny days (25°C–28°C) and cool, comfortable nights (13°C–18°C).

== See ==

=== Monuments and Heritage ===
- Charminar: Built in 1591, this four-minaret monument stands 56 meters high at the bustling heart of Old Hyderabad. Overlooking the historic Laad Bazaar, its intricate stucco ornamentation and first-floor mosque showcase Indo-Islamic Qutb Shahi architecture.
- Golconda Fort: Sprawling 16th-century fortress renowned for its acoustic engineering (a clap at the entrance dome can be heard 1 km away at the Bala Hissar pavilion), royal residences, and diamond vaults that once held the Koh-i-Noor and Hope diamonds. The evening sound-and-light show narrates its rich history.
- Chowmahalla Palace: The lavish 18th-century official residence of the Nizams, inspired by the Shah of Iran's palace in Tehran. Highlights include the Grand Khilwat Mubarak Durbar Hall with 19 Belgian crystal chandeliers and vintage royal automobile collection.
- Qutb Shahi Tombs: Majestic domed mausoleums set in landscaped Ibrahim Bagh gardens, honoring the seven Qutb Shahi sultans who founded the city.
- Salar Jung Museum: One of the three National Museums of India, housing the vast personal art collection of Salar Jung III, including the famous Veiled Rebecca marble sculpture and 19th-century musical bracket clock.

=== Lakes, Parks and Culture ===
- Hussain Sagar Lake: A large 16th-century artificial lake featuring a monolithic 18-meter-tall white granite Buddha statue on Gibraltar Rock, illuminated boat rides, and the scenic Necklace Road promenade.
- Birla Mandir: A serene modern temple constructed entirely from 2,000 tons of pure white Rajasthani marble atop the 85-meter-high Naubat Pahad hill, offering panoramic views of Hyderabad and Secunderabad.
- Ramoji Film City: The world's largest integrated film studio complex (certified by Guinness World Records), spanning over 1,600 acres on the city's outskirts.

== Food and Dining ==
Hyderabad is an UNESCO Creative City of Gastronomy:
- Hyderabadi Dum Biryani: Fragrant basmati rice layered with marinated tender goat mutton or chicken, saffron, fried onions (birista), and mint, slow-cooked sealed in a deg with dough. Famous venues include Paradise Biryani (MG Road), Bawarchi (RTC X Roads), Cafe Bahar (Hyderguda), and Shadab (Near Charminar).
- Haleem: A rich, slow-cooked stew of wheat, barley, shredded meat, lentils, and spices, traditionally prepared during the holy month of Ramadan.
- Irani Chai and Osmania Biscuits: Sweet, creamy tea served in classic glass tumblers with crumbly salted butter biscuits at Nimrah Cafe & Bakery opposite Charminar.
- Royal Fine Dining: The Jewel of Nizam (The Minar at Golkonda Resort, Gandipet) and Bidri (Marriott) for historic Nizami banquet cuisine.
